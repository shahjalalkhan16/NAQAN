# NAQAN: Noise-Aware Quantum Attention Network

## Project Description
This project implements a Noise-Aware Quantum Attention Network (NAQAN) for image denoising. The model leverages quantum computing principles within its attention mechanism to enhance its ability to remove various types of noise (Gaussian, Speckle, Poisson) from medical images (OCTMNIST dataset) while preserving structural integrity. The model dynamically adapts its denoising strategy based on the estimated noise level in the input image.

## Model Architecture
The NAQAN model is designed with several key components:

*   **Quantum Channel Attention (QCA):** This module integrates quantum computing principles to create a noise-aware attention mechanism. It uses a quantum circuit (`q_circuit`) with `n_qubits` (8 in this implementation) where input features are encoded as rotation angles (`RY`) and trainable parameters (`RZ`) are applied. CNOT gates create entanglement, and the expectation values of PauliZ operators provide the quantum output. This output is then fed into classical layers to generate channel-wise attention weights.

*   **Noise Embedding:** A small neural network that takes a scalar noise level as input and generates `gamma` and `beta` parameters for noise modulation. This allows the model to adapt its processing based on the severity of the noise.

*   **Noise Modulation:** Applies the `gamma` and `beta` parameters generated by the Noise Embedding to the feature maps of the network, effectively modulating the features based on the noise level.

*   **NAQAN Block:** The core building block of the network, comprising two convolutional layers, ReLU activation, a QCA module, and a Noise Modulation layer. It operates in a residual learning fashion.

*   **Full NAQAN Model:** An encoder-decoder-like structure. It starts with convolutional layers (`enc1`, `enc2`) to extract features, passes them through the `NAQANBlock`, and then uses decoder-like convolutional layers (`dec1`, `dec2`) to reconstruct the clean image by estimating and subtracting the noise.

## Dataset
The model is trained and evaluated using the **OCTMNIST** dataset, which is part of MedMNIST. This dataset consists of optical coherence tomography (OCT) images, primarily focusing on medical imaging applications. The images are grayscale and normalized to a range of [-1, 1].

## Noise Types and Levels
To evaluate the model's robustness, three common types of noise were artificially injected into the images:

*   **Gaussian Noise:** Additive noise with a normal distribution.
*   **Speckle Noise:** Multiplicative noise, often found in radar and medical ultrasound images.
*   **Poisson Noise:** Photon shot noise, common in low-light imaging or X-ray imaging, where noise follows a Poisson distribution.

Each noise type was applied at three different levels of intensity:

*   **Levels:** 0.05, 0.1, 0.2

## Evaluation Metrics
The performance of the denoising model is quantitatively assessed using two widely accepted metrics:

*   **PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio):** Measures the quality of reconstruction of noisy images. A higher PSNR generally indicates a higher quality reconstructed image. It is expressed in decibels (dB) and is inversely related to the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the original and processed image.

*   **SSIM (Structural Similarity Index Measure):** Evaluates the perceived quality of denoised images by considering luminance, contrast, and structural information. SSIM values range from -1 to 1, where 1 indicates perfect similarity. It is often a better indicator of perceptual quality than PSNR.

## Performance Analysis Findings
The NAQAN model demonstrates strong denoising capabilities across various noise types and levels, with consistently high SSIM values indicating good structural preservation. Key findings include:

*   **Gaussian Noise:** PSNR degrades with increasing noise levels (from ~31.58 at 0.05 to ~24.90 at 0.2), as expected. However, SSIM remains high (~0.9980 to ~0.9907), suggesting effective structural preservation even under high Gaussian noise.
*   **Speckle Noise:** The model excels at denoising speckle noise, achieving the highest PSNR at lower levels (~33.31 at 0.05). PSNR decreases with higher levels (~28.53 at 0.2), but SSIM remains exceptionally high (~0.9986 to ~0.9960), highlighting superior visual quality and structure retention.
*   **Poisson Noise:** A remarkable strength is the model's stability and effectiveness against Poisson noise. Both PSNR (~30.51-30.55) and SSIM (~0.9974) values are highly consistent across all tested levels, indicating robust performance largely independent of noise intensity.

## Visualization Results
The visualization grid clearly demonstrates the model's denoising effectiveness:

*   **Gaussian Noise:** Visually, denoised images degrade with increasing noise, showing more residual noise and blurring of fine details at higher levels. This aligns with the decreasing PSNR values (e.g., PSNR ~32.91 at 0.05 vs. ~25.60 at 0.2).
*   **Speckle Noise:** The model effectively removes speckle noise, producing visually clean images at lower levels and maintaining good detail preservation at higher levels, despite subtle texture persistence. High PSNR values (e.g., ~33.31 at 0.05) correlate with this strong visual quality.
*   **Poisson Noise:** The most striking visual aspect is the model's consistent performance. Denoised images for all Poisson noise levels (0.05, 0.1, 0.2) are remarkably similar to the clean image, with the grainy texture effectively suppressed and details preserved. This visual stability is strongly supported by the highly consistent PSNR values (e.g., ~30.51 at 0.05, ~30.55 at 0.2).

Overall, the model visually excels in preserving image structure and details, particularly against speckle and Poisson noise. Higher levels of Gaussian noise represent a more challenging scenario visually, where some finer details might be lost.
